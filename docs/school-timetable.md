# School timetable

Step 1 gives the office period sets, optional locations, a class's week and computed clashes. Step 2 adds the teacher portal's read-only **My schedule**. Step 3 adds the office's four read-only **Schedules** views and schedule-only printing. Families have no timetable routes or screens. No courses, credits, rotating days, automatic placement or exports are included.

## Switch and roles

`school_timetable` is a school grant, default false for every organisation, hidden from payloads, catalogue, history and navigation while off. Its routes return 404 even to a SuperAdmin while off. The audited `CapabilityWriter` refuses enabling it outside a school or without `school_calendar`. A combined write may enable both. Dated terms and class subjects are optional. The calendar cannot be switched off while the timetable remains on.

The office roles are the calendar's roles: MasjidAdmin with membership in the addressed school and SuperAdmin. The calendar has no separate view/edit permission; neither does the timetable. The admin and tenant middleware fence every route before the timetable gate. Teacher and family tokens cannot enter this realm.

## Reference data

A named period set belongs to one school year. Its rows have stable IDs, names, ordered positions, school-local start/end clocks and `teaching` or `block` kinds. Clocks never undergo timezone conversion. Rows must end after they start and cannot overlap within a set. Blocks may carry activities or whole-class meetings; subjects require teaching rows.

Each meeting weekday uses one school set; a class may override it. Meeting weekdays come from `SchoolDateAuthority`, including the calendar's single-weekday fallback while dated terms are off. A set change carries existing meetings to the new set's rows by position for the entire year; it refuses missing positions or subject meetings mapped to blocks. Times and row order also apply to the whole year. The screen says “This changes the times for the whole year.”

Locations belong to the school, with a case-insensitive unique name, optional positive capacity and active flag. Inactive locations remain readable but cannot be newly assigned. `timetable_class_rooms` holds usual locations, keeping `groups` unchanged. A meeting's NULL room resolves through this table. Usual location changes are undated and affect every meeting resolving that room, including past views.

## Dated meetings

A meeting names one class, weekday and period row, with exactly one of a class subject, a named activity of at most 60 characters, or the whole class where class subjects are off. Subject choices must belong to the class and be visible. Teachers are live Teacher staff accounts with a teacher membership at this school, independent of their class assignments. Defaults offered come from subject assignments, otherwise the class's teachers. Subject and whole-class meetings require at least one teacher; activities may have none.

Creating requires `effective_from`, offered as school-local today or the year's first day, whichever is later. Optional `effective_until` must be within the year and on or after the start. Changing later closes the previous row the day before and creates another. A change or removal on or before its start hard-deletes the original row if it has never been in effect on an earlier open school day of its own weekday. A change inserts the corrected meeting; a removal leaves no meeting or teacher junction behind and frees the period/set. School-local yesterday, the meeting's stored weekday and effective range, year bounds and closure dates determine earlier occurrences. Current calendar weekday changes cannot erase a retained meeting's history. A meeting with an earlier open occurrence keeps the existing dated-history behavior: on/before-start changes edit it in place, later changes version it, removals only shorten it (including an empty range when dated before its start).

Two rows for the same class, weekday and period cannot overlap in dates, even with clash confirmation. Copying a day uses another weekday of the same set, defaults to the same teachers/room, and opens the copied meetings from the chosen date through the year. Copy is atomic, including validation and warning failures.

## Clashes and reads

A first placement/change with clashes returns 409 `{status: "clashes", clashes: [...], clash_fingerprint: "..."}`. Each clash has `kind`, `id`, `name`, weekday, intersecting dates, and both meeting descriptions with class, label, teachers, room and clock times. The 409 includes `clash_fingerprint`, an HMAC of the normalized submission and canonical clash list. The screen resends the exact reviewed body with `confirm_clashes: true` and that fingerprint. If the body or clashes change, including when clashes disappear, a fresh 409 replaces the list and nothing is saved. Day copies use one fingerprint for the complete request/list and roll back all tentative rows on a warning or refusal. Confirmation never bypasses validation or duplicate refusal.

Teacher and resolved-room clashes compare overlapping clocks on the same weekday across sets, plus effective date ranges that contain that weekday. Touching boundaries are allowed. `GroupMembership::joiningDate` reuses the model's existing `joined_at` date cast, which the office attendance clip and roster moves already read. The protected legacy attendance method stays verbatim; timetable fallback dates use `membershipStart`. Student clashes use participant Contact identities on both rosters, intersecting each membership's joining date (the office's `joined_at`, then move-in date, then creation date) and leaving date (exclusive). Guardian edges do not count. The Clashes panel computes outstanding clashes as of the chosen date, including clashes introduced by whole-year setup changes.

`TimetableReader::meetings(school, year, date, lens, id, weekday)` is the shared server reader for `teacher`, `class`, `room` and `school`; it returns the week as of the supplied date, with optional weekday filtering. It executes exactly two queries regardless of class/meeting/teacher counts. `clashes` adds one bulk roster query, for exactly three. The teacher schedule also uses this bulk reader through `teacherVersions`, then resolves each displayed date against its own school year and effective range. The class week renders independent weekday columns with their own times, rather than a forced common row scale.

## Routes

Base `/api/admin/masjids/{masjid_id}/timetable`:

| Method | Suffix | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| GET | `/` | School-local today and available years |
| GET | `/years/{year_id}/setup` | Bulk setup, classes, subjects and teacher choices |
| GET | `/years/{year_id}/week?group_id=…&as_of=…` | Class week (date defaults to today) |
| GET | `/years/{year_id}/clashes?as_of=…` | Computed clash list |
| POST / PUT / DELETE | `/years/{year_id}/sets[/{set_id}]` | Create, edit complete ordered rows, remove set |
| PUT | `/years/{year_id}/days` | Submitted school/class weekday mappings; NULL class mapping restores school's set |
| POST / PUT / DELETE | `/years/{year_id}/rooms[/{room_id}]` | School room reference data |
| PUT | `/years/{year_id}/classes/{group_id}/room` | Set/clear usual location |
| POST / PUT / DELETE | `/years/{year_id}/meetings[/{meeting_id}]` | Place/change/end dated meeting |
| POST | `/years/{year_id}/copy-day` | Atomic copy to same-set weekdays |

Every addressed year, class, set, period, subject, room and meeting is tenant-scoped; set/period/meeting IDs also resolve within the addressed year, subjects within the class. Submitted teachers resolve through this school's staff membership. Year writers lock the organisation then year and recheck the switch. The class Location writer takes the organisation lock and needs no year. GET readers do not write.

## Retention and scrub

Sets/period rows with any meetings cannot be deleted, and period removal names the number held. Deleting or archiving a class, deleting a class subject's actual row, deleting or changing the access of a referenced teacher account in school or global account operations, and removing its school membership are refused to preserve references, including historical rows. Hiding a subject through the existing office Remove action ends its meetings from school-local today, preserving earlier rows. Restoring that subject does not restore its timetable automatically. Rooms with explicit or usual-room meetings are kept. A school year with period sets is kept; year date/weekday edits cannot strand its timetable.

The hidden `has_timetable_records` booleans on `masjids` and `users` are retention hints, independent of grants. Their migration backfills existing rows; timetable row/link writers maintain them and deletions refresh them. A request-local positive never skips a durable marking write: an earlier nested transaction may have rolled back. The school hint reuses the existing request organisation memo; the account hint travels on the account already loaded by global routes. Missing hints before migration read as false, without schema probes. An unused OFF school and unreferenced global accounts preserve the base SQL list and payloads. Retained records are protected after switch-off. The flags are not PII-shaped; the staging scrub coverage gate confirms no new classification is required. Existing raw database snapshot comparisons assert the new flags are zero, then compare every legacy column against the unchanged fixtures; these internal columns do not enter public payloads.

There are eight additive tenant-scoped tables, all with `id` primary keys and short named indexes/FKs. No new `groups` column and no membership foreign key. The staging scrub's `name` token matches set/period/room names, room `name_key` and `activity_name`; all are anonymised conservatively, since typed names can identify people.

## Dated range and retained-data rules

Removal with a past open occurrence only shortens a range; an earlier existing end stays. A change after the selected row's end refuses: “This meeting has already ended before that date. Choose the meeting live on that date.” It never silently operates on another row. A read between an old and new version has one row per slot.

Class archive/deletion, actual subject deletion, referenced account archive/deletion or access removal, referenced school membership removal, location deletion and year deletion use the same worded retention refusal after switch-off. Subject hiding while ON ends meetings from today; a hide while OFF is reconciled when switching back on. New timetable endpoints still answer 404 while OFF. Teacher removal with timetable ON and class subjects OFF takes the organisation mutex as its first transaction statement, ahead of the account lock and reference checks; an unused OFF school's statement list stays as on the base.

Both live capability writers reconcile retained ongoing/future rows while holding the organisation mutex before switching on. Hidden/missing subjects, archived/inactive/nonclass classes, teachers no longer live in the school, weekdays no longer met and missing/mismatched period-set mappings each **end the affected meetings from school-local today**, keeping earlier history. Existing earlier ends are never extended. The single-switch response adds `data.timetable_ended_meetings`; the bulk response includes that count in `meta`. The switch panel reports the count and that earlier rows were kept. There is no automatic reopening on later restore. Clearing an invalid override or restoring a weekday does not recreate ended meetings.

Submitted teacher IDs never select a lens: the authenticated teacher ID is the only one used.

The grid has independent day columns sorted by clock time across period sets, rather than a shared period-ID scale. Empty periods and unassigned school blocks are absent. Columns include days with this teacher's live meetings, today when inside the selected week, and calendar closure days on meeting weekdays. ## A lesson two classes take together (2026-10-13)

**The case.** Al-Razi's PE is Monday and Wednesday at 2:00 PM, and one teacher takes both classes at once. The timetable stores one lesson per class, so the office saved two lessons with the same teacher at the same time and confirmed a clash. The clash list then showed two clashes that are not mistakes, and the teacher's own schedule showed two lessons at once.

**Data shape.** One nullable column, `timetable_meetings.joint_key` (`char(36)`, a UUID minted by the server), and one index, `tt_meetings_joint (masjid_id, joint_key)`. Lesson rows that carry the same key are one lesson their classes take together. No new table, model, switch, permission or capability. The key never leaves the server: `TimetableMeeting` hides it and `TimetableReader` removes it from every row it returns.

**The rule lives in one place and is applied when reading.** Two rows are *together* when they carry the same key **and** belong to different classes of the same school year, on the same weekday, with the same start and end clocks, **and** their effective dates meet (on a dated read: both are in effect on that date). `TimetableReader` works this out for every lens and adds `joint` to each row that has such partners: `[{id, group_id, group_name, effective_from, effective_until}]`, ordered by class name. A row with no partner carries no new key at all, so a school that never joins a lesson receives exactly the rows it received before. `TimetableReader::compare` skips a pair that is together, for every clash kind: one lesson cannot clash with itself, whether the thing shared is a teacher, a location or a student on both rosters.

Because the rule is read, not stored, a key the facts no longer support is inert instead of wrong. Changing a period set's clocks, moving a weekday to another set, ending or removing one class's lesson, or a later dated version on another period: none of them has to clean anything up, and none can leave a key hiding a real clash. A third class at that time with the same teacher is not part of the lesson, so it clashes with each class of the joint lesson. Organisation and year cannot be crossed: every read is one organisation's rows, the key is unique across the table, and the rule compares the year.

**Rejected.**
- *A `timetable_joint_lessons` table with a foreign key.* A ninth tenant table to carry through the retention hints, the deletion guards, the staging scrub and the tenant-isolation suite, to hold a row with no fields of its own.
- *One lesson row with a class junction.* The truthful shape for a module started today, but it changes the one-row-per-class-slot rule that every reader, the class week editor, copy-day and all the guards are built on. Not small, and not shippable in a day.
- *Pointing at an anchor lesson's id.* A dated change replaces the row it would point at, and the first class to leave would orphan the rest.
- *An integer label taken from a row id.* A lesson that leaves one joint lesson and starts another would reuse its own id and silently rejoin its old partners.
- *Inferring it from "same teacher, same time, same name".* The office has to say so. A real double booking would otherwise hide itself.

**Writing.** All three writers take the organisation and year locks the other timetable writers take.
- `PUT /years/{year_id}/meetings/{meeting_id}/joint` with `{"meeting_ids": [..]}` says the named lessons are taken together with this one. Each must be a lesson of this school year (anything else is 404), of another class, on the same weekday at the same clocks, with dates that meet this lesson's (each a worded 422). The lessons end up sharing one key; if any of them was already part of a joint lesson, the two joint lessons become one. It changes the rows in place, with no date and no new version, the way a class's usual location is undated: it is how existing lessons are fixed without re-entering them. A lesson that already has a dated change waiting is two stored rows (same class, weekday and period, a later first date); what the office says of a lesson holds for those later rows too, of this lesson and of each named one, so the clash does not come back on the later row's first date.
- `DELETE /years/{year_id}/meetings/{meeting_id}/joint` takes this class's lesson out, with its later dated versions that carry the same key, as joining took them in. The others stay together; a class left alone reads as an ordinary lesson. Earlier rows are history and are left as they were.
- `GET /years/{year_id}/meetings/{meeting_id}/joint` returns the lesson, the lessons it is taken with, and the other classes' lessons at that weekday and time that it could be taken with.
- `POST` and `PUT /meetings` accept `joint_with: [meeting ids]`. The save is checked with those lessons already together, so the clash that prompted it is gone and any other clash is still reported and still needs its own confirmation. The lessons the review treats as together are exactly the rows that will carry the saved lesson's key (its kept key, the named rows, their later versions, and every row already sharing a key with any of them), held to the reader's own rule (`TimetableReader::sameLesson`), so the review reports what a read reports afterwards and nothing else. A dated change that keeps the weekday and period carries the key to the new version; one that moves the lesson drops it. Copying a day never copies a key: the copy is a new lesson on another weekday.

**Screens.** When a save meets a clash with another class's lesson at the same time, the review lists those lessons as tick boxes and offers **These classes take this lesson together** beside **Save anyway**. Only the ticked lessons are named: a clash at the same time can be a real one (another teacher's class in the same hall), and a clash left unticked stays a clash. One lesson at that time is ticked already, so the common case is one click; with more than one, or after a joint choice has been made, nothing is ticked for the office. The Clashes list offers the same button on a pair at the same time, and the button's accessible name says which two classes. A lesson's own dialog says who it is taken with, takes it out, or lists the other classes' lessons at that time to choose from. Before the office asks, it knows the partners in effect on the date shown; once asked, it shows every partner on any of the lesson's dates (a class joined from a later date reads "(from 2 November 2026)") and can take the lesson out. Joining and taking out act on the stored lesson and close the dialog, so both wait, with a sentence saying why, while the form has unsaved changes (the "From which date" box included), and the section says that they do not apply from that date. Reading the list is not saving: the form keeps its Save button, and focus moves to the first class to choose, or to the sentence that says there is none. Every schedule names the classes together: a class's own views say **With {class}** under the lesson, and a list that would show the same joint lesson twice (a teacher's day, a location's day, a teacher's row of the whole-school grid) shows it once.

**Who sees it.** The office's class week and its four Schedules views, and the teacher's My schedule. Families have no timetable route or screen, and no other class view reads lessons, so nothing else needed changing. A teacher's merged slot keeps the first class's row as the slot and lists the others in `joint` with their own link (`href`) when that teacher is placed with them too, and no link otherwise; the co-teachers of every merged row are named once. The office's By teacher view is the same slot with every link removed. One function does the merging for every such list (`TimetableReader::asOneLesson`): the kept row gains the other classes' teachers, a different lesson name ("PE / Games") and a different place, so a location's day shows a teacher placed only with the second class.

**Queries.** Unchanged for a school with no joint lesson, pinned at the old counts. A whole-school read already holds every partner. A read through a narrower lens (one class, one teacher, one location) that holds a joint lesson asks once more, for the other classes' rows of those keys, whatever the number of classes: 3 instead of 2 in the reader, 13 instead of 12 for the office class week.

**Not carried, on purpose.** Copying a day copies lessons, not the join (the copy is on another weekday, where the other class may have nothing). Joining and taking out are undated: to separate two classes from a date, change the lesson from that date first, then take the new row out (the earlier weeks stay one lesson). A third class that clashes with a joint lesson is reported once per class of that lesson, as two ordinary clashes. The teacher portal is English-only, so "With" is not translated.

**Evidence.** `SchoolTimetableJointLessonTest` (15 cases: a joined pair across all three clash kinds and every view; a third class; refusals across organisations, years, weekdays, times, classes and dates; token and switch fences; taking out, editing, moving and deleting; effective dates and a set change; copy-day; the save-time choice with what remains; the teacher's one lesson; later dated versions on joining, taking out and saving; a save reviewed against the lessons it will be joined with; a location's day with every class's teachers; reads and ordinary saves before the migration has run; query counts at 2 and 12 classes; the literal key lists of an ordinary row and answer). `timetable-joint-lesson.test.ts` mounts the office screen, the whole-school grid and My schedule (13 cases). Each rule in the reader and each branch in the screens was removed in turn and a test failed; the same-class exclusion, which no office action can reach, is pinned with a forged row.

**Release order.** `bin/deploy` puts the code in place before it migrates. Until `joint_key` exists the reader treats it as absent and an ordinary save does not write it, so every timetable read and ordinary lesson save works in that window (tested by rolling the migration back inside a test). The three `/joint` routes and a save that names `joint_with` need the column.

**Checked for MySQL.** The migration uses Blueprint only: `uuid()` and a named index, no raw SQL, on a table that already has its `id` primary key. Its MySQL statements were produced with the MySQL grammar in pretend mode and read (recorded in `DECISIONS.md`, 2026-10-13): backtick-quoted identifiers, which `ANSI_QUOTES` accepts, and an index name of 17 characters against the 64-character limit SQLite does not enforce. It was not run against a MySQL server here; CI's MySQL job does that.
